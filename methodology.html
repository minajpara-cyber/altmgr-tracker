Methodology and data dictionary

How the tracker turns public filings into comparable fund-level research. Values are estimates from automated extraction and should be checked against the source filing before investment use.

Coverage

The universe covers non-traded BDCs and REITs, interval and tender-offer funds, perpetual private-equity and infrastructure vehicles, and selected partnership products. Manager views exclude sub-advised partnership vehicles from directly managed AUM; the Universe and Strategy pages retain them.

Sources and freshness

Quarterly data comes primarily from SEC EDGAR 10-K, 10-Q, N-CSR/N-CSRS, and N-PORT filings. Monthly data also uses 8-K and NPORT-P disclosures. “Financials through” is the latest represented financial period; “refreshed” is the site build date. The Filings page separately shows the newest indexed filing.

Effective NAV

For non-traded REITs, the tracker prefers manager-published fair-value Fund NAV over GAAP equity. When a separately reported DST sleeve exists, it is added to Fund NAV for totals. Other products use period-end NAV, falling back to total net assets or equity when necessary.

Returns

“Total return — best available” follows a source hierarchy: fund-reported share-class return, then N-PORT monthly returns compounded (the lead share class’s, meaning the class with the lowest fees, usually Class I; or, where N-PORT can’t say which class that is, the highest-returning class), then derived per-share total return, NAV-bridge return, and operating return. A source that moves more than 15% in a quarter is passed over as a likely extraction error (typically a year-to-date figure read as one quarter’s), except N-PORT’s class returns: the fund states them month by month, so they only need to stay within 50%. Where a fund’s N-PORT class returns cannot be tied to its lead share class (Hamilton Lane’s funds), an unidentified quarter is left blank rather than filled from the NAV bridge or operating return, which are fund-level and move with subscriptions. Where a filing’s monthly figure is not the month’s total return at all (HL Scope RIC filed months that leave out a distribution or run from the wrong month-end NAV), the month is corrected from the fund’s own reports — the distribution in its N-CSR, its NAV per share — where they show the right figure, and otherwise left blank with its quarter; that fund’s months after the last one checked stay blank until they are checked. N-PORT filings don’t name the class behind each return. Because the lead has the lowest fees, it is taken to be the class that out-earns every other over the quarter by at least 0.03 percentage points, more than the rounding of the filed figures. Where the fund’s own reports show the lead was a different class, that class’s filed return is used: eight quarters so far, at KKR’s KABF, Brookfield’s BIIF, CION Ares’s CADC and StepStone’s SSINF and SSCIN. Apollo’s ADCR shows Class I, about 90% of net assets, from Q4 2022. The class this rule would take there, Class F, is closed to new money and under 1% of net assets today, and has printed 2-3 percentage points a year above Class I since 2023, because the adviser caps its total expenses, interest included, at 1.50%. ADCR’s filings don’t name their classes either, so each quarter’s Class I figure is checked against the fund’s reports, and a new filing’s months stay blank until it has been, with the quarter using the next class below Class F. Those reports also show that one series, KABF’s Class D before it had shares, was no shareholder’s return, so it is left out. In a new filing, a class that comes out on top but doesn’t track any of the others month by month, as a fee difference would, is not taken for the lead: those months stay blank until someone checks them (the Monthly Filing Scrape tab can still show them from a manager’s own document or a provisional workbook row), and the quarter uses the next class. A class can also lead by a cent of NAV per share, which can’t be told from a fee difference without the fund’s reports, so any quarter’s return can be another class’s, usually by about 0.1 percentage points or less. YTD, TTM, and trailing three-year figures compound the period returns in their window; a window spanning a period without a return, after the fund’s first return, is left blank, as are all of a fund’s figures when its latest period has none. The three-year figure is cumulative, not annualized.

Monthly returns for funds that report only a monthly NAV per share (most non-traded BDCs and REITs) are derived, normally from the Class I series: the month’s NAV change plus the distribution whose record date falls in that month, which is not always the one announced alongside that NAV. Each NAV sits at the month-end its filing says it was struck, even when the filing is the price for a later month’s subscriptions. Distributions come from the fund’s 8-Ks and, where those are silent, the distribution tables in its 10-Qs and 10-Ks. A month is shown as a NAV change alone only when the fund paid nothing that month (accumulating share classes, quarterly payers between record dates, months before a fund’s first distribution); a month whose distribution cannot be found is left blank rather than shown without it.

N-PORT monthly gains

The Monthly tab shows what a registered fund’s NPORT-P states for each month beyond its flows: net realized gain and change in unrealized appreciation on investments (Item B.5.d) and on derivatives (Item B.5.c, all asset categories together), and, at each quarter’s report date, total assets, total liabilities and net assets (Item B.1). They are shown as filed. Total gain adds the four monthly lines up and is not itself a filed figure.

These figures are unaudited, and the statement of operations in the fund’s N-CSR is the record. Over the 168 half-years and years with a statement on file, N-PORT’s total gain was within 1% of average net assets of the statement’s in 69% of them and within 2% in 88%. Compare totals rather than the two lines: some funds move amounts between realized and unrealized when they characterize distributions at year end. N-PORT’s net assets can also differ from the audited figure at a fiscal period end (by 0.5% at the median), and a fund’s year-end marks can reach N-PORT in a later month, as they do at Hamilton Lane. Ares’s APMF runs ahead of its statements, by $847M against $515M for the year to March 2026. The funds furthest from their statements carry a note on the page. A figure the fund’s own reports show is wrong, or one over a third of net assets, is left out and listed under the fund’s table. That is six cells today, across ADCR, CTAC, KABF and PGUSPE, one of them a $4.9B loss on FX forwards in a fund with $12.5B of net assets.

Each return record in Item B.5.a is a share class’s, but filings don’t name the class and don’t always keep the records in the same order, so N-PORT’s classes can’t be published under their own names. Class high and class low are the highest and lowest of the month’s records, leaving out a record that printed 0 beside the others’ returns, one the fund’s reports show no shareholder earned, and three class months at KABF filed without their distribution. A month before a fund began shows no return, even where its filing prints 0.00. Month return stays the lead share class’s, where the rules under Returns identify it, and its Return source column says whether it came from N-PORT, was derived from NAV per share, or came from a factsheet. Hamilton Lane’s funds show no class range, because their records don’t reproduce the Class I series they report.

Flows

Net flow equals cash subscriptions plus DRIP and applicable DST proceeds, less redemptions and applicable DST cash payouts. Flow percentage divides this activity by beginning-of-period NAV. OP-unit conversions from a DST sleeve are internal reclassifications and are not treated as new external capital. A registered fund’s monthly redemptions are its NPORT-P’s. A 10-Q filer’s come from the filing’s repurchase or tender table: the printed amount, or shares times price where a table prints only the size of the offer.

Debt

A REIT’s total debt is the sum of the borrowing lines on its balance sheet: mortgages, credit facilities, term loans, unsecured notes and secured financings of its debt investments. DST program financing obligations and the obligations of consolidated securitization trusts are not borrowings and are left out.

Fees

The effective management-fee rate annualizes the disclosed period fee against the relevant fee base. REITs generally use fair-value Fund NAV plus any DST sleeve; other vehicles use period-end NAV. Rates outside a plausible range are a data-quality signal, not automatically an economic conclusion.

Derived periods

A “derived Q4” is calculated from the full-year filing less Q1, Q2, and Q3 where the filing does not provide a standalone fourth quarter (for a September year end the derived quarter is Q3). A year-to-date figure is used only if it adds up: one that shrinks during the year, exceeds the full year, or repeats a single quarter is set aside, and the quarter is then the full year less the three filed quarters. Semiannual and annual ’40 Act rows remain labeled H/FY. Interpolated values are visually marked in detailed tables.

Feeder and Aggregator funds

Some perpetual funds invest through an Aggregator that also holds parallel vehicles’ money, and their filings print the feeder’s, the fund’s and the Aggregator’s statements side by side. Blackstone’s BXPE and BXINF are shown at the Aggregator, which pays the management fee and performance allocation, so NAV, flows, income and fees cover the whole pool. Per-unit NAV and returns are the fund’s own. BXPE Lux and BXINFRA Lux invest through separate Luxembourg aggregators and are tracked on their own.

Blackstone’s BXHF (BXMS) is built the same way. Its monthly NAV and sales are already the pool’s; its quarterly figures follow once a filing prints the Aggregator’s statements.

TPG’s TPOP is also shown at its Aggregator for NAV, flows, income and net change from operations, but TPG charges its fees above the Aggregator, in TPOP, its tax-exempt feeder and a parallel vehicle. No filing prints the pool’s fees. TPOP’s page therefore gives TPOP’s own printed fee, expense and net investment loss lines, and two more rows, marked as estimates, for the whole pool. The management fee estimate applies the contract rates to the Aggregator’s average net assets in each class: 1.25% on Class I, 1.00% on Class R-I and nothing on Class F. The performance allocation estimate divides TPOP’s line by its interest in the Aggregator. Manager and strategy totals use the printed lines, so they understate TPOP’s fees. The Aggregator’s redemptions include withdrawals to pay fees and expenses, so they overstate what investors redeemed. TPOP’s monthly subscriptions are the whole pool’s from October 2025, and its monthly NAV is TPOP’s own.

Limitations

Funds disclose on different calendars and with different definitions. Missing values remain blank; they are not zero-filled except where an explicitly additive stacked chart requires a continuous axis. Automated parsing can misidentify a table or unit, so source links and filing dates are included for verification.
